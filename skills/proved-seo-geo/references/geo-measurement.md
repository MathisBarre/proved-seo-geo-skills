# GEO/GIO and measurement

GEO/GIO in this skill means applying sound technical and editorial SEO so a page may be eligible for AI-powered search experiences. It is not a separate optimization recipe.

## Google

Google says its AI features use the same SEO fundamentals. There are no special AI-only technical requirements or special schema, and Google explicitly says `llms.txt`, “chunking,” rewriting for AI, and inauthentic brand mentions are not required. A supporting page must be indexed and eligible for a snippet, while still meeting normal Search requirements. `[G-AI-FEATURES] [G-AI-OPTIMIZATION]`

Use those statements to remove unsupported work from a roadmap, not to promise inclusion in an AI Overview or AI Mode response.

Where it is available to a property, Google's Generative AI performance report reports organic impressions from supported Google Search generative features and can group data by page, country, date, and device. It is not a citation log, and report access/data availability are limited. `[G-AI-PERFORMANCE]`

## Bing and Copilot

Bing frames its webmaster fundamentals as relevant to Bing and AI experiences. Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance data can report observed grounding/citation activity. Treat it as an outcome measurement: it does not identify a causal factor, guarantee a click, or prove the factual quality of the generated answer. Do not assume it supplies a per-answer audit trail or dimensions it does not expose; explicitly mark unavailable measurement granularity. `[B-GUIDELINES] [B-AI-PERFORMANCE]`

## Choose the outcome before changing the page

Track the outcomes separately:

| Outcome | Example evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Technical eligibility | crawl/index status, rendered HTML, sitemap acceptance |
| Organic search visibility | impressions, queries, landing pages, rankings where appropriate |
| AI visibility | Google generative impressions by the dimensions the report exposes; Bing AI Performance records by the dimensions it exposes; unavailable granularity marked explicitly |
| Brand visibility | explicit brand mentions, separately from URL citations |
| Business impact | qualified referrals, engagement, conversion, assisted revenue |

Secondary research finds that AI results and their citations can be volatile and that citation, naming, clicks, and conversion are distinct. Use that evidence to choose a measurement design, not a content formula. `[S-SE-RANKING-VOLATILITY] [S-PEW-CLICKS] [S-SEMRUSH-GHOST-CITATIONS]`

## Experiment protocol

When primary guidance does not resolve a choice, design a bounded test:

1. State the visitor problem and a falsifiable hypothesis.
2. Define the target page group, market, engine/surface, baseline period, and success metrics.
3. Change only what the team can describe and audit.
4. Preserve a comparison group or a credible before/after window where possible.
5. Report results, confidence limits, competing explanations, and any harms.
6. Keep the result labeled as a local experiment unless it has broader support.

Never use a single AI answer, one dashboard spike, or a vendor correlation as proof that a formatting choice causes a citation.
